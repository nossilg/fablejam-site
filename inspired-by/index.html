The fable tellers

Aesop

The storyteller of ancient Greece, as the legend goes: a man who couldn't speak until a kindness gave him stories. Nobody knows for sure if he was real, but his fables are, and people have told them for more than two thousand years.

  • Aesop's fables in old English translations (Wikisource)

The Panchatantra

A very old book of animal stories from India, told by a wise teacher to young princes. Our Season 1 opener, how Jet and Crumb met, comes from its doves, crow and mouse.

  • The Panchatantra, translated by Arthur W. Ryder (1925)

Plutarch, Pliny and Plato

Writers from long ago who tell some of our fables, or the things in them, in their own books: the Moon who wanted a dress that fit, the thirsty crow and her stones, and Socrates in the shade of a plane tree.

  • Plutarch, The Dinner of the Seven Wise Men (LacusCurtius)
  • Pliny the Elder, Natural History, book 10 (Perseus)
  • Plato, Phaedrus, translated by Benjamin Jowett (Project Gutenberg)

Jean de La Fontaine

The French poet who turned fables into poems more than three hundred years ago, and said he owed many of them to Pilpay, a wise man from India.

The old books

Aesop's Fables, 1912

A new translation by V. S. Vernon Jones, with pictures by Arthur Rackham. This is "our old book": its pages and Rackham's paintings open our histories.

  • The text (Project Gutenberg)
  • The whole book, scanned, with Rackham's colour plates (Internet Archive)

The Aesop for Children, 1919

Fables retold for children, with pictures by Milo Winter.

  • The text (Project Gutenberg)

Chambry's Greek fables, 1925

Émile Chambry's edition of the Greek fables, for the ones in neither old English book.

  • The Travellers and the Plane-Tree, Greek and French (Wikisource)

The music, and the makers

Aesop Rock

One of Jet's favourite rappers, and the reason she keeps a Word Jar: he plays with words the way Aesop played with stories. He isn't involved with Fable Jam; we just love his way with words.

  • aesoprock.com

Willy and Dolly

Our sister project: a comic about two little helpers and the human who types to them, made the same way, with lots of care and a little help from AI. It's written for grown-ups.
